# Round 3: final judge accessibility

Verbatim final report from this subagent.

1) RANKING: B, D, A, E, C

2)
**B. Say It Right**: 8/10, about 22% to win Best Overall. Worst flaw: the stranger study has no control. A second try after 15 seconds of practice gets better even without a sticker, so the gain will look bigger than it is. The live demo also depends on a phoneme model reading the judge's own name through a headset in a loud hall. Fix: split testers into two groups, second try with the sticker versus second try with only the written respelling, and alternate which name each tester gets. Name one real owner in the pitch.

**D. Elastic**: 6.5/10, about 11%. Worst flaw: second-language students in lectures mostly use live captions. The team hasn't shown that the 8 testers want slowed, lagging audio more than captions, and the paper they cite found that slowing speech doesn't improve understanding. So the need may be invented. Fix: in hour 1, interview the 8 testers. Add a captions arm to the blind A/B test, and report honestly which one they would choose for class.

**A. Heckle**: 6/10, about 9%. Worst flaw: two avatars are second-language speakers who drift when the speaker goes fast. That behaviour comes from a pace rule, not from those people's own marks, so it builds in a stereotype under a real person's name. Also, 12 people each marking 8 transcripts is too little data for per-person models or meaningful precision and recall. Fix: drop the language labels. Set each listener's pace limit from their own data, and grow the survey to 20+ people marking 15 transcripts each.

**E. Monkey's Paw**: 5/10, about 5%. Worst flaw: there's no beneficiary, and 60 hand-written curses run out fast. A second wish starts repeating jokes, and free-text wishes that miss the fixed vocabulary will frustrate players. Replay is the whole case, and it's fragile. Fix: lead with the starter chips. Let players submit curses that the team reviews and adds live. Report the replay rate on 40+ strangers even if it's under half.

**C. Proven Guard**: 5.5/10, about 8%. Worst flaw: no human user has been met. "0 of 412 attacks got through" is true by construction, because a proven guard blocks by definition. And a Dafny proof of the daily-window rule in 28 hours is a real risk. Fix: lead with the round-2 heist on the AI-written guard. Have a member of the named team say on camera what the guard caught in their real agent.

3) HEAD TO HEAD: B beats D because the name's owner is standing at the table and decides directly whether an attempt was right. People in the building will actually ask for stickers. D's benefit is contradicted by its own cited paper and has to compete with captions.

4) LOSES IF: the syllables, pitch line or respelling come out visibly wrong on the judge's own name in the loud hall, or the study shows sticker gains no bigger than plain practice.
