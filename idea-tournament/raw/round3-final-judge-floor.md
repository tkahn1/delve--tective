# Round 3: final judge floor

Verbatim final report from this subagent.

RANKING: A, B, D, E, C

A. Heckle: 8.5/10. Win chance about 22%.
Worst flaw: it is close to MinervAI (simulated audience, 2023 winner). A tired judge may think "we already gave this prize to that." The loud hall also puts the headset demo at risk, and typed mode is much less fun.
Fix: open with "MinervAI's students were made up; our 12 are real people," and point to Maya's actual survey sheet on the table. Test the mic at the expo spot, not just at the venue.

B. Say It Right: 8/10. Win chance about 17%.
Worst flaw: wav2vec2 phoneme output on non-English names in a noisy hall could be garbage. If my own name comes back wrong, the magic is gone and it feels like NameCoach.
Fix: make the pitch line and syllable split (pure signal processing, solid) the 5-second moment. Present the respelling as "draft, you fix it," and show one named hacker whose sticker is on another table.

D. Elastic: 6.5/10. Win chance about 9%.
Worst flaw: 15% slower through earbuds in a loud hall is subtle. I can't feel it working in three minutes, and the 3-second lag behind lips sounds like a bug.
Fix: open on an extreme A/B (a really fast speaker), let the judge flip the switch themselves, and put the strongest of the 8 ESL testers' quotes on screen first.

E. Monkey's Paw: 6/10. Win chance about 6%.
Worst flaw: typing into a phone and getting a witty card back reads as "isn't this just a prompt?" The 60 hand-written curses make the solver invisible, and the jokes wear thin after two.
Fix: make the glowing gap word the hero animation and show the solver's live elimination of curses. Lead with the replay-rate number.

C. Proven Guard: 5.5/10. Win chance about 4%.
Worst flaw: I'm moderately technical. Dafny, Z3 and three rounds don't fit in three minutes, and the try-it part is typing into a chat box. It is impressive, but I can't feel the proof.
Fix: cut to one moment: the AI-written guard gets robbed on screen, the proven guard says "no attack exists." Then aim it at the security tracks, not the grand prize.

HEAD TO HEAD: Heckle shows a big, visible change within a second, to any sentence I say, and it's fun. It is also hard to break. Say It Right is more moving, but its core depends on fragile phoneme recognition, and one mangled judge name kills it.

LOSES IF: the hall is too loud for the headset and it drops to typed mode. Then twelve faces reacting to pasted text feels like a lexicon lookup, and MinervAI comparisons win.
