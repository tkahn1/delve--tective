# Project list (name and one-sentence pitch)

0. Verified AI Code: A plugin for AI coding tools that turns a plain-English request into a formal specification, writes the code, then produces a machine-checked proof that the code meets the specification, or a concrete counterexample when it doesn't.
1. Vault: Type a money rule for a bank account in plain English and Vault either proves no transaction can ever break it, or drains a mock account to show you the hole.
2. Monkey's Paw: Make a wish, and a genie powered by a logic solver grants it in the worst way that still matches your exact words.
3. Who Can See This?: Describe who should see what in plain English and get a proven answer to questions like "can a student ever see another student's grade?"
4. Tripwire: Write a sneaky sentence into an email, watch a normal AI banking agent obey it and send your money away, then watch the same agent with Tripwire refuse.
5. Ghost Imports: Ask an AI for code, and Ghost Imports highlights every software package it made up, which an attacker could register as malware today.
6. Blacklight: A UV flashlight for text: shine it on anything you're about to paste into an AI and the invisible instructions glow.
7. Glance: Type a sentence using only your eyes and a laptop webcam; AI guesses the rest of the phrase, so you look at about 3 targets instead of 30 letters.
8. Proof of Typing: Your keystroke rhythm proves you composed an answer yourself instead of retyping a ChatGPT draft from a second screen.
9. Tell: A two-player bluffing game where the webcam reads each player's pulse from tiny colour changes in their face and shows it to the opponent.
10. Wand Duel: Two judges scan a QR code and their phones become wands; they cast spells by drawing gestures in the air and duel on the big screen.
11. Feel the Graph: Drag your finger across a chart on your phone; higher values buzz harder and sound higher, so you can find the peak and the trend with your eyes closed.
12. Choir of One: Hum 5 seconds into headphones and hear yourself come back as a 4-part choir, every part in your own voice.
13. Flipbook: Tell it two things and it paints one picture that shows the first when upright and the second when you turn your phone upside down.
14. Window: Take a selfie and your phone becomes a window: move your head and the photo shifts in true 3D, as if the scene were inside the phone.
15. Shadow Zoo: Make a hand-shadow animal in front of your phone camera and it detaches from your hand and walks around the screen as its own creature.
16. Swarm Screen: Judges drop their phones on the table in any arrangement; one laptop camera works out where each phone is and a single animation flows across all of them.
17. Face Duet: Two people play one song together using only their faces: one player's eyebrows set the pitch, the other's mouth shapes the sound.
18. Elastic: Live lectures in your earbuds, slowed down 15%, catching up with the speaker by cutting the pauses, so you never fall more than about 3 seconds behind.
19. Tired Test: A 60-second tap test on your phone that tells you how many drinks your sleep loss is worth.
20. Heckle: Pitch to a crowd of animated judges who visibly get bored, check their phones and walk out the moment you say jargon, ramble or mumble.
21. Blind Spot: Explain your bug out loud like you would to a rubber duck, and your code lights up with the lines you skipped, because the bug is usually where you didn't look.
22. Idiom Lens: Live captions that colour idioms, sarcasm and cultural references, with a one-tap literal meaning.
23. Say It Right: Say your name once and get a badge QR code that teaches anyone to say it: your own voice slowed down, syllables drawn as stress bars, and an English-friendly respelling.
24. Calm Race: Two people look into a laptop webcam that reads their pulse from their faces and shows it as a live heat map; the first to bring their heart rate down wins.
25. Mind Charades: Taboo played against an AI's brain: make the AI think "pizza" without saying pizza, and a meter reads the thought straight from its neurons.
26. Was That Me?: Record 10 seconds of your voice, hear a clone of yourself you can't tell apart, then watch an invisible watermark catch the fake even after it plays through a phone speaker.
27. Did It Read Me?: Paste any paragraph and see, word by word, whether an AI language model memorised it during training.
28. Shaky: An AI answer where every sentence the model is likely making up visibly wobbles.
29. Minuteman Mirror: Say a costume out loud and your live webcam reflection becomes it in about a second.
30. Coin Jar: Your bank balance becomes a jar of coins on your phone, and to pay you tilt the phone and pour real-feeling coins into the shop's cup.
31. Vish: An AI scam caller phones you as "your bank" and tries to talk you into moving money from a mock account, while a live panel names each manipulation trick as it uses it.
32. Legend: Point your phone at any chart, map or slide, tap a colour in the legend, and everything that colour pulses on camera.
33. Sticky Note: Write a sneaky instruction on a sticky note, slap it on any object, and watch a camera-driven AI shopping agent get hijacked by it, then watch a shield catch it.
34. Stand-ins: Snap a photo of a user interface you sketched, and five AI "users" with simulated impairments (blurry vision, shaky hands, screen reader only) try to use it while you watch their cursors.
